Why we resole instead of replace

By the Aurelia materials team · 12 min read · Updated 14 July 2026

A running shoe rarely fails all at once. The upper is usually fine at 700 km. The laces are fine. What goes is the outsole rubber under the forefoot, and after that the midsole foam loses about a fifth of its rebound. Throwing away the whole shoe because eight grams of rubber has worn through has always struck us as a strange way to build a product.

So in 2024 we changed the construction. Every Aurelia model now uses a stitched-and-cemented joint rather than a fully bonded one, which means a workshop can separate the outsole without destroying the upper. It costs us roughly $6 more per pair to make. It saves a customer $129 and keeps around 1.1 kg of mixed-material waste out of the ground.

The programme is deliberately unglamorous. You send the pair back in the box your last order arrived in, we inspect the upper, and if it passes we fit a new outsole and midsole and return them inside eleven working days. About 6% of pairs fail inspection, almost always because of heel-counter damage from being pulled off without unlacing.

What surprised us most was the resale effect. Resoled pairs hold about 40% of their original value on secondhand markets, against 12% for worn pairs of comparable shoes with bonded soles. Durability, it turns out, is a pricing feature rather than a cost centre.

We are extending the programme to the Track Spike line in the autumn, which is harder because the plate is load-bearing. If it works, every shoe we sell will be repairable.
